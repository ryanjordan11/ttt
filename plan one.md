## Funnel Plan

This version keeps the downloadable guide, adds a short quiz so the visitor gets a small win, and removes the equal split between “shop” and “book a call.”

The goal is to make the funnel feel guided, not open-ended.

Instead of ending with two equal choices, the funnel should lead people toward the primary outcome:

**Book a call with Timoteo**

The PDF guide still matters, but it becomes part of the value stack, not the final goal.

The flow should feel like this:

1. The visitor feels understood
2. The visitor opts in for something useful
3. The visitor answers a few quick questions and gets a sense of progress
4. The visitor gets the guide
5. The visitor is directed toward booking a call

This creates more momentum and makes the call CTA feel earned instead of abrupt.

## Why This Structure Is Stronger

The current “choose your path” ending weakens the funnel if the real goal is getting people onto a call.

If calls are the primary conversion, the funnel should guide people toward that result instead of making them stop and decide between two equal actions.

The quiz improves the flow because:

- it creates engagement
- it gives the visitor a small psychological win
- it makes the experience feel more personalized
- it gives context for the recommendation at the end
- it helps the final CTA feel natural

The guide should stay because it provides immediate value and reinforces trust.

The guide should be:

- promised on the landing page
- unlocked after the email opt-in
- available for immediate download
- also sent by email automatically

So the guide becomes:

- the opt-in incentive
- the educational bridge
- the trust-builder
- the follow-up asset

## Positioning Direction

The client’s newer positioning is stronger than the earlier toxin-only framing.

The best parts of it are:

- helping people understand what is actually happening inside their body
- helping symptoms make sense instead of feeling random
- helping people identify what is interfering
- creating progress by removing what is working against them

That said, the message should stay grounded and concrete.

Words like “patterns,” “interference,” and “internal environment” can work, but they should be tied to everyday realities like:

- products people use daily
- environmental stressors
- routines and habits
- hidden toxic load

The tone should feel:

- warm
- premium
- trustworthy
- clear

Not:

- overly clinical
- overly corporate
- hype-driven
- fear-heavy

## Step 2: Micro-Quiz

This should be short. No more than 3 questions.

It should feel like a quick diagnostic, not a long quiz.

### Quiz Intro Copy

**Headline:**
Before You Access The Guide, Answer 3 Quick Questions

**Subheadline:**
This helps us understand where you may be feeling the most strain so we can point you in the right direction.

### Quiz Questions

**Question 1:**
What feels most frustrating for you right now?

**Answer options:**
- Low energy or feeling run down
- Brain fog or mental heaviness
- Ongoing symptoms that do not seem to make sense
- Feeling overwhelmed by what to change first

**Question 2:**
What do you feel you need most right now?

**Answer options:**
- Clear guidance on what may be interfering
- A simple place to start
- Help understanding which products or habits may be contributing
- A personalized next step

**Question 3:**
How are you most likely to move forward?

**Answer options:**
- I want a clear recommendation from someone who understands this
- I want to review the guide first and take a first step
- I want help making sense of what my body may be responding to
- I am ready to talk through what to do next
